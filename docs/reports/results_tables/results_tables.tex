{\Large\bfseries PIIGuard: performance of every model, questions 1 to 3}\\[3pt]
{\small Key result tables \textperiodcentered{} CSED490E, PIIGuard team \textperiodcentered{} November 24, 2026}
\end{center}

\textbf{Setup.}
Test sets: ai4privacy (10,000 sentences), Nemotron-PII (2,000 documents) and Gretel (2,962 documents).
Micro scores with strict matching (same start, end and label). The \emph{leak rate} is the share of gold PII
values that reach the language model unmasked. Comparisons: \textbf{A} the six labels of the proposal (main);
\textbf{B} without names and addresses; \textbf{A-dates} and \textbf{B-dates} (comparison C) score every date
instead of the date of birth.
\emph{Noisy copies} (question 2): about one edit per ten words, of one kind (typo, space, case, punct, number) or of
all five (mixed), seed 1; the gold spans follow the text. \emph{BERT-FT} (question 3): the benchmark's BERT
fine-tuned on the ai4privacy train split (115,533 records), on clean records only or on clean records plus one
mixed-noise copy of each, with the same settings and steps; scores are the mean of three training seeds. The two
versions are compared with each other only, not with the off-the-shelf detectors (proposal, Appendix B).
\emph{Statistics}: 95\% intervals from a paired cluster bootstrap (10,000 draws); paired approximate
randomization tests (10,000 rounds for question 1, 20,000 for questions 2 and 3), Holm's correction within each
family. A difference is \emph{established} when the corrected $p<0.05$, its interval excludes 0 and it is at
least 0.01.

\inshort{
\item GLiNER has the best F2 on ai4privacy and Nemotron-PII. On Gretel, BERT and GLiNER are level in F2 (GLiNER is
  ahead in F1), and BERT is ahead once every date is scored (A-dates, 0.787 against 0.595). Presidio finds no
  names or addresses (F2 0.16 to 0.43).
\item BERT-FT is almost perfect in its own domain (ai4privacy, F2 0.986) but falls below the original BERT on Gretel,
  mostly through addresses: ai4privacy marks only the building number and street.
}

\inshort{
\item For BERT and GLiNER, missing or extra spaces cost the most on every dataset, and typos come next except for
  GLiNER on Gretel. On typo, space and mixed noise BERT loses more than GLiNER on all three datasets (intervals of
  \textit{BERT $-$ GLiNER} below 0).
\item Lowercase text does not change BERT at all (its tokenizer lowercases) but hurts GLiNER on ai4privacy
  (F2 $-$0.028, leak rate $+$3.0 points).
\item Number noise barely matters in comparison A, where only dates of birth are scored, but breaks dates when every
  date is scored (A-dates): Presidio $-$0.241 and BERT $-$0.066 on Nemotron-PII.
}

\inshort{
\item In its own domain (ai4privacy), training on the noisy copies wins back two thirds of the mixed-noise loss
  ($+$0.047 of 0.071) for a clean cost of 0.003, detectable but below 0.01.
\item On the other two datasets, BERT-FT clean+noisy is better on every test set, clean text included
  (Nemotron-PII $+$0.068, Gretel $+$0.026), and leaks less (Nemotron-PII mixed 24.6\% $\to$ 12.9\%). All these
  differences are established.
}
